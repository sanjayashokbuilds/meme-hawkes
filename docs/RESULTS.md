## Stage 5b: launch-and-fade background

Generated by `scripts/fit_fading.py`. 34 tokens refitted with a launch-time background that fades, μ(t) = μ₀ + μ₁·e^(−γt). Simulation shows a fading burst can make the constant model nearly double n ([validation](VALIDATION_FADING.md)).

| | Constant background | Fading background |
|---|---|---|
| Median branching ratio n | 0.953 | 0.582 |
| Tokens passing the fit check | 26% | 56% |
| Two-type model passing the fit check | 8% | 48% |
| Median early→ordinary branching | 0.372 | 0.147 |

- The fading model is preferred by AIC for 82% of tokens.
- n falls by a median of 0.193 when the launch burst is modelled (Wilcoxon signed-rank p = 1.1e-05).
- Median launch-burst half-life: 39 s.

**Pump-and-dump tokens vs the rest, under the fading model:**

| Measure | Pump-and-dump tokens | Median | Other tokens | Median | Mann-Whitney p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| n_fading | 26 | 0.571 | 8 | 0.743 | 0.288 |
| early_to_ordinary_fading | 20 | 0.155 | 5 | 0.124 | 0.869 |
| burst_half_life_s | 26 | 39.169 | 8 | 45.828 | 0.563 |

Caveat: the noise bar for early→ordinary (0.121) was calibrated for the constant-background model; the fading model has more parameters, so its bar for a single token is likely higher.

## What the results say so far

Written by hand after Stages 4–5b; every number comes from the sections above.

1. **The headline Stage 4 numbers were inflated by a modelling artefact, and Stage 5b shows it.** With a constant background rate, the median branching ratio was 0.95, which looks near-critical. Allowing interest to fade after launch (median half-life 39 s) cuts it to 0.58, roughly doubles the share of tokens that pass the fit check, and is preferred by AIC for 82% of tokens. Simulation confirms the mechanism: a fading launch burst alone pushes a true n of 0.4 up to about 0.79. This is the "apparent criticality" Filimonov & Sornette warned about, observed on a new market.
2. **Early-slot wallets do appear to excite ordinary trading, but less than first estimated.** Early→ordinary falls from 0.37 to 0.15 under the better-fitting model. It's still positive, but close to the single-token noise level, so it should be read as "small and uncertain".
3. **None of the excitation measures separate pump-and-dumps from other tokens.** Every comparison has p > 0.25. With only 8 non-pump-and-dump tokens, that is low power, not proof of no difference.
4. **As an early warning, the rolling branching ratio does not beat a simple price baseline.** It does better than chance at low false-alarm rates (58% of dumps warned, 36% false alarms), but "the price has already doubled from its low" warns of 81% of dumps at the same false-alarm rate. The comparison is partly stacked in the baseline's favour, because the label itself is defined by price. Even so, the Hawkes signal adds no demonstrated value. Two more reasons for caution: n estimated from 30 trades is very noisy (see the simulation line in Stage 5), and many dumps happen before 30 trades have accumulated.
5. **What would change these conclusions:** a larger sample with more non-pump-and-dump tokens; signals built on the fading model; and labels not defined by price (for example, an independent list of known rugs), so the baseline comparison isn't circular.
