| 27 | 5 | **Early-warning design (fixed before any signal was computed; only the labels' timing had been looked at):** at every 5th trade, compute signals from the *preceding* trades only. A token's *alarm* is the first time a signal crosses its threshold. For a pump-and-dump, the alarm counts only if it comes **before the dump time**, and lead time = dump time − alarm time. For other tokens, any alarm in the first hour is a false alarm. Signals: (1) branching ratio n fitted to the last 30 trades; (2) trade rate over the last 30 trades; (3) price ÷ lowest price so far; (4) share of the last 30 trades that are early-wallet sells. Pre-set operating points: n ≥ 0.9, rate ≥ 2 trades/s, price ≥ 1.5× low, early-sell share ≥ 0.2; full threshold sweeps also reported. Compared with the base rate (26 of 40 tokens are pump-and-dumps, so "always alarm" has 65% precision) | Dumps come fast (median 43 s after launch), so signals use trade counts, not minutes. The price signal is a deliberately tough baseline: the label is defined by price, so a price ≥ 2× alarm precedes every dump by construction, and the useful question is whether anything gives a *longer lead time at a comparable false-alarm rate* | Time windows in minutes (most dumps would be over); choosing thresholds after seeing results (optimistic) |

## Known limitations so far
- **n < 1 by construction.** A pump could genuinely be explosive (n ≥ 1) for a while. Estimates pressing against 1 should be read as "at least near-critical", not as exact values.
- **Pooled stream.** Stage 3 treats all trades as one stream. Splitting by wallet group and buy/sell is Stage 4.
- **Sample size per token.** With ~300 events the branching ratio's spread is about ±0.06 (see `VALIDATION.md`), so small differences between single tokens aren't meaningful.

## Open questions
- Tied timestamps: Solana times are slot-level (~400 ms), so many trades share a time. Method to be chosen in Stage 3.
- Within-slot order: we keep the order the RPC returns. To verify against a block explorer on a few trades.
- Credits per token lookup: expected about 1 per transaction fetched; to measure on real data.
- Sniper-bot spam touches the bonding curve without trading, so the sampling filter's "≥100 transactions" partly counted spam. Handled by #24 rather than re-drawing the sample.
- Wash trading: some tokens have hundreds of trades from 4–6 wallets. Stage 4 must stop these loops being read as crowd excitation.
- Bot churn: on the first real token, 120 trades came from 15 wallets, one buying and selling every second or so. The early-slot rule labels such wallets "ordinary". Consider a third group (very-high-frequency wallets) in Stage 4.
